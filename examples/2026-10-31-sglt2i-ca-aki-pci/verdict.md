# Medical Research Council Verdict: SGLT2 inhibitors to prevent CA-AKI after elective PCI

## 3. Veto status
**Evidence vetoes A as written.** Harm: dapagliflozin started in SGLT2i-naive T2DM patients with eGFR 30-60 across a fasting, contrast-exposed procedure, with no DSMB, no stopping rules, and no ketone monitoring, while one cohort links SGLT2i use to higher CA-AKI risk (PMID 42212353).
**CLEARS IF**: (1) independent DSMB with a pre-set harm boundary for AKI and ketoacidosis; (2) capillary ketones before PCI and daily to 72 h, with a stop rule on raised ketones or loss of oral intake; (3) a hydration and drug-hold protocol for the fasting window; (4) exclusion of insulin-deficient patients, low-carbohydrate diets, and eGFR below 30; (5) written confirmation of what happened to NCT04806633; (6) registration as a pilot, or re-powering as a multicentre trial; (7) baseline creatinine and cystatin C before the first dose, with a pre-specified rule for the drug's own early creatinine rise.
Ethics: no veto, but would veto if a registry with protocol-required blood draws ran under a consent waiver.

## 4. Where the council agrees
- The question is open and worth answering: no adequately powered RCT with a clinical endpoint exists, the observational evidence points both ways (PMID 39392560, PMID 38179307 vs PMID 42212353), and there is no trial in Egypt or Africa.
- A single-centre efficacy RCT of 300 patients cannot answer it (all five seats after peer review).
- B cannot settle efficacy because of confounding by indication and prevalent-user bias. Its value is the local event rate, eligibility, and how often 48-72 h creatinine is actually measured.
- Doing nothing (C) gives no local evidence; NCT04806633 may never report.

## 5. Clashes and tradeoffs
- **Rigour vs feasibility.** Methods and Evidence want randomisation now; Feasibility, Patient, and Ethics say the team cannot deliver it yet. Resolved by sequencing: measure first, then randomise at the right size.
- **Safety vs burden.** Evidence's ketone monitoring and DSMB make A safer; Patient and Feasibility point out that patients go home the next day and nobody is funded to run the checks. Still open: the safeguards must be costed and staffed before A is submitted.

## 6. Caught in peer review
- **The drug's own creatinine rise** (raised independently by 3 reviewers, missed by all 5 in Round 1): starting an SGLT2i 3 days before PCI can itself meet KDIGO creatinine criteria, so the primary endpoint would be misclassified differently in each arm. Fix the baseline before the first dose and add cystatin C.
- **Post-trial duty of care**: SGLT2i-naive T2DM patients with eGFR 30-60 usually have an untreated indication. Stopping the drug after 6 days without referral is a duty-of-care problem, and the eligibility rule selects undertreated patients.
- **Consent for the registry**: once blood draws are protocol-required, the prospective arm needs written consent and likely falls under Law 214/2020 (expert judgment); a waiver covers only the retrospective arm.
- **Too few users for B**: if few diabetic PCI patients were on an SGLT2i in the last 2 years, matching will leave too few to analyse. Count them first.
- **Removed claim**: one card described "5.4-13.1%" as a local event-rate range; in PMID 37276980 these are the user and non-user rates. Not used in this verdict.

## 7. Conditions
1. Split B into two arms in one protocol: (a) a retrospective 2-year audit under an REC consent waiver with coded extraction and a data-protection plan under PDPL 151/2020; (b) a 3-4 month prospective registry of every diabetic elective PCI patient, with written consent and a protocol creatinine draw at 48-72 h (local lab, transport reimbursed).
2. B's stated aims are the local CA-AKI rate, the number eligible for A, the consent rate, and 72 h endpoint completion. Not efficacy.
3. Count SGLT2i users in the audit window before any comparative analysis.
4. Name a statistician (part-time is fine) and a successor for the resident before data collection.
5. Agree with the REC in advance whether patients found to have unfollowed AKI will be recontacted.
6. Only then write A: registered as a pilot RCT, or as a multicentre trial with about 780 patients if the control rate is near 12% and RR 0.5 is targeted (Methods' calculation), with all seven veto conditions and a referral to start long-term SGLT2i therapy at the end of the trial.

## 8. First step
Pull the cath lab log for the last 2 years and count three numbers: diabetic elective PCIs, how many had creatinine measured at 48-72 h, and how many were on an SGLT2i. That one afternoon of work tells you whether B and A are possible at all.

## 9. Key evidence
- PMID 39392560 - Meta-analysis, mostly observational: RR 0.48 for CI-AKI with chronic SGLT2i use.
- PMID 42212353 - 2026 cohort, n=2,082: SGLT2i use linked to higher CI-AKI risk (matched OR 2.38); local rate 11.7%.
- PMID 39890841 - Only peri-procedural RCT, n=121: surrogate endpoints only.
- PMID 38179307 - Meta-analysis whose authors call the evidence inconclusive.
- NCT04806633 - 1,722-patient RCT, status unknown, no results.
