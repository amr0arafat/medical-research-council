# Round 2: anonymous peer review

Map (not shown to reviewers): Response 1 = Feasibility, 2 = Evidence, 3 = Patient, 4 = Methods, 5 = Ethics.

## Evidence (reviewing; own card = 2)
STRONGEST: 4 - Its power arithmetic shows n=300 detects only RR≤0.3. A null result from Option A would tell us nothing, yet it would still expose about 150 fasting, contrast-exposed patients with eGFR 30-60 to a drug with a possible harm signal (PMID 42212353). Running a trial that cannot answer its question is a patient-safety and ethics problem as well as a statistical one.
BLIND SPOT: 5 - It claims B will give A its "safety case". A retrospective cohort of chronic users cannot show whether starting the drug fresh in SGLT2i-naive patients, around a fasting procedure, is safe. That new-start window is where euglycaemic ketoacidosis and volume depletion would happen. Leaning on B for safety reassurance could lower the safeguards for A.
ALL MISSED: Starting an SGLT2i causes an expected early rise in creatinine as the kidney's filtration pressure drops (expert judgment). Option A starts the drug 3 days before PCI, so the active arm may meet KDIGO creatinine criteria from the drug itself, not from contrast injury. That could hide real protection or look like harm. The trial needs a baseline creatinine taken before the first dose, a measurement on the day of PCI, and cystatin C as a co-primary or sensitivity endpoint. No response raised this, nor the timing of stopping metformin in the same eGFR band.
UNSUPPORTED CLAIMS: Response 4: "SPIRIT 2025 / CONSORT 2025" not labelled; its sample sizes (355/arm, 780, 2,200) are derived calculations (they check out) but are not labelled. Response 1: "most elective PCI patients go home before 72 hours" and "B in about 6 months" not labelled. Response 2 (mine): "interim look after 100 patients" not labelled.
REVISED SCORES: A=4.0, B=5.0, C=3.0
REVISION REASON: Response 4 showed that A as written is underpowered, so its harms are not balanced by information gained. Responses 1 and 3 showed that a prospective registry with protocol-mandated creatinine at 48-72 h makes B useful for event rates and endpoint completion.
VETO: Unchanged (still triggered on A). Two conditions are added to the original five: (6) register A openly as a pilot or feasibility RCT, or re-power it as a multicentre trial; (7) take baseline creatinine and cystatin C before the first dose, and pre-specify how the drug's initiation creatinine rise will be handled in the AKI endpoint.

## Methods (reviewing; own card = 4)
STRONGEST: 1 - It names the measurement failure that sinks both designs. If elective PCI patients go home before 48-72 h creatinine is drawn, B's event rate and effect estimate are biased by who gets tested. A's primary endpoint would also have informative missingness, and Response 3 notes this would fall on the highest-risk patients. Its hybrid fix (a prospective screening log with a protocol creatinine draw) directly measures the inputs a sample-size calculation needs.
BLIND SPOT: 5 - It assumes B will "give A the equipoise and safety case". B compares chronic prevalent users with non-users, so it cannot tell us the safety of starting the drug for 6 days in SGLT2i-naive patients. PMID 42212353 shows that propensity matching did not even settle the direction of effect (OR 2.38 against the RR 0.48 in PMID 39392560). Scoring B at 8.0 on that basis overstates what B can deliver.
ALL MISSED: SGLT2i initiation itself causes an early haemodynamic rise in creatinine (expert judgment). In A, the drug starts 3 days before PCI. If baseline creatinine is taken at PCI, the drug arm's baseline is already shifted. If it is taken before the drug, part of the drug's own creatinine rise could meet KDIGO criteria. Either way the primary endpoint is misclassified differently in the two arms, and the bias could run in either direction. The protocol must fix the baseline creatinine as pre-drug and pre-PCI. It should make cystatin C a co-primary or key sensitivity endpoint (expert judgment).
UNSUPPORTED CLAIMS:
- R4 (mine): the sample sizes (about 355 per arm, about 780 and about 2,200 in total) are my own calculations and were not labelled as such. Rechecked, they hold at alpha 0.05 and 80% power. The SPIRIT 2025 and CONSORT 2025 citations are not in the pack.
- R1: "about 6 months" to complete B is not labelled.
- R2: the "interim look after 100 patients" is not labelled.
- R5: Law 214/2020, PDPL 151/2020 and PACTR are not in the pack, though R5 does label the regulatory route as expert judgment.
REVISED SCORES: A=5.5, B=4.5, C=2.5
REVISION REASON: Responses 1 and 3 convinced me that B, reframed as an audit of endpoint completeness and the local event rate, has more design value than I first allowed. It still cannot answer the efficacy question, so A stays unchanged and must be re-powered or registered as a pilot.

## Ethics (reviewing; own card = 5)
STRONGEST: 4 - It shows that n=300 at RR 0.5 cannot answer the KDIGO question. Running it as an efficacy trial would expose patients to risk with no answerable question, which is an ethics and integrity problem, not only a statistical one. Its fix, registering openly as a pilot feasibility RCT, is the honest way to register it.
BLIND SPOT: 1 - Its "hybrid B" adds a creatinine draw at 48-72 h that the protocol requires. Once the protocol mandates a procedure, this is no longer a records review. It needs prospective written consent and probably falls within the scope of Law 214/2020 (expert judgment). "Consent where required" understates this, and the REC waiver I proposed for B cannot cover that arm.
ALL MISSED: In T2DM with eGFR 30-60, being "SGLT2i-naive" usually means the patient has an untreated guideline indication (expert judgment). In A, the eligibility rule picks out undertreated patients. Stopping a 6-day course with no plan to continue it raises a post-trial-access and duty-of-care question, so the protocol should require referral to start chronic therapy. In B, non-users are a sicker or lower-access group, which adds confounding beyond what the pack's gaps describe. The retrospective review may also find AKI that was never followed up. The REC needs a pre-agreed rule on whether to recontact those patients.
UNSUPPORTED CLAIMS: Response 4's sample sizes (355/arm, ~780, ~2,200) and "RR ≤0.3 detectable" are calculations not labelled expert judgment, though I could reproduce them arithmetically. "SPIRIT 2025/CONSORT 2025" is not in the pack. Response 1's "about 6 months" for B is unlabelled. Response 2's interim look at 100 patients is unlabelled.
REVISED SCORES: A=4.5, B=7.5, C=4.0
REVISION REASON: The power analysis in Response 4 lowers A, because an underpowered efficacy trial is hard to justify ethically. Response 4's selective-ascertainment point and Response 1's missing-creatinine risk limit what B can claim, so B should be framed as an event-rate and feasibility study. Response 3 convinced me that C has a real equity cost.
VETO: Unchanged (none). It applies on a condition: if Response 1's prospective registry arm, with its protocol-required draws, runs under B's consent waiver, I would veto it as research without lawful consent.

## Patient (reviewing; own card = 3)
STRONGEST: 4 - At n=300, A can only detect RR ≤0.3. That means every patient would take six days of study drug, fast with possible ketone risk, and come back for blood tests, all for a null result nobody could interpret. Asking patients to carry that burden in a trial that cannot answer its question is an ethical problem, not only a statistical one. Its fallback, registering A openly as a pilot feasibility RCT, also matches the consent, return-visit and retention data I asked for.
BLIND SPOT: 2 - It wants daily capillary ketones up to 72 h and a DSMB, but it assumes patients stay in hospital and that a team with no trial coordinator can staff this. Many elective patients go home the next day (expert judgment). So this safety fix adds return visits, and the patients most likely to miss them are poorer, rural and older, the ones most at risk. The safety plan would then fail exactly where it is needed.
ALL MISSED: (1) Dapagliflozin started 3 days before PCI causes a haemodynamic creatinine rise. If baseline creatinine is taken before the first dose, the drug alone could trigger KDIGO "AKI" and bias the result against it. Baseline timing has to be specified (expert judgment). (2) SGLT2i-naive patients with T2DM and eGFR 30-60 usually have a long-term indication for the drug. The trial stops it after 3 days and has no plan for post-trial access or a hand-off to long-term prescribing. Enrolling them also selects patients whose care already leaves out an indicated drug (expert judgment).
UNSUPPORTED CLAIMS: Response 4 cites "SPIRIT 2025/CONSORT 2025"; its sample-size numbers (355/arm, 780, 2,200) are its own calculations, unlabelled, though they check out arithmetically. Response 2's interim look "after 100 patients" is unlabelled. Response 1 gives "about 6 months" for B and "5.4–13.1%" as a local event-rate range; the second misreads PMID 37276980, where those are the user and non-user rates. Response 5 cites Law 214/2020, PDPL 151/2020 and PACTR; these are covered by its blanket expert-judgment label.
REVISED SCORES: A=4.0, B=6.5, C=3.0
REVISION REASON: Response 4's power calculation shows that A at n=300 would expose patients without being able to give an answer. B stays unchanged because of Response 1's point about missing 48-72 h creatinine.

## Feasibility (reviewing; own card = 1)
STRONGEST: 4 - Its power calculation settles the decision on feasibility grounds. At the 11.7% control rate in PMID 42212353, a relative risk of 0.5 needs about 780 patients, and an RR of 0.7 needs about 2,200. The planned n=300 at one site could detect only an RR of 0.3 or lower. That makes single-centre A unfinishable as an efficacy trial, not just hard to run. It leaves two honest routes: a multicentre trial, or a trial registered as a pilot.
BLIND SPOT: 2 - It clears A by adding a DSMB, an interim look, and daily ketone checks to 72 h. It never asks who does this work. The team has no coordinator, no statistician, and no secured drug or placebo. Daily ketones to 72 h also means an inpatient stay or home testing kits for patients usually discharged the next day (expert judgment). Each safeguard adds cost and staff time, and nothing in the brief funds them.
ALL MISSED: Option B depends on how many patients were exposed. Nobody asked how many diabetic PCI patients at this hospital were actually on an SGLT2i over the 2 years. If uptake was low, propensity matching leaves too few users to estimate anything (expert judgment). Staffing is also fragile. A resident is the only data workforce, and residents rotate or graduate within the 2-year window. Without a named successor, the work will lose continuity (expert judgment).
UNSUPPORTED CLAIMS: Response 4: "SPIRIT 2025/CONSORT 2025" is not in the pack. Its sample sizes are its own calculation, not citations; I checked them and they are arithmetically sound. Response 5: Law 214/2020, PDPL 151/2020, the EDA, the Supreme Council, and PACTR are not in the pack and are covered only by a general expert-judgment label. Response 1 (mine): the "about 6 months" for B is not explicitly labelled as expert judgment.
REVISED SCORES: A=2.5, B=7.0, C=3.0
REVISION REASON: Response 4 showed that routine 48-72 h creatinine is drawn selectively in sicker patients. That biases both B's event rate and its effect estimate. It also confirmed that n=300 is futile, which pushes the realistic route for A further out.
