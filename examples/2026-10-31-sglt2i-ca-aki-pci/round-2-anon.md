# Round 2 input: anonymised Round 1 responses

Options are A, B, C (see the brief). Responses are numbered 1 to 5.

## Response 1
STANCE: B. It is the only option this team can finish with what it has, and it measures the two numbers that decide whether A could ever be finished: how many patients are eligible and how often CA-AKI happens here.
SCORES: A=2.5, B=7.5, C=3.0
ANALYSIS: Option A fails on resources before it fails on science. There is no statistician, no trial coordinator, no secured dapagliflozin or matching placebo, and only a small internal grant. Placebo-matched drug supply alone usually takes 6–12 months to arrange through a manufacturer or compounding pharmacy (expert judgment). Getting a drug study approved under Egyptian clinical research law and registered within 4 months is unrealistic (expert judgment). Recruitment does not add up either. About 400 diabetic PCIs a year shrinks to perhaps 80–120 once you require eGFR 30–60, elective PCI and no current SGLT2i, and to fewer still after consent (expert judgment). That gives well under 300 patients in 18 months at one centre. Even NCT04806633, planned for 1,722 patients, has unknown status and no results. Most elective PCI patients also go home before 72 hours, so the primary endpoint needs funded outpatient creatinine draws, which are not budgeted. Option B uses the cath lab log, the EHR and the lab system, so it needs no drug supply. It can be done in about 6 months with a resident plus part-time statistics support. The local CA-AKI rate matters a great deal: PMID 42212353 reports 11.7%, PMID 37276980 reports 5.4–13.1%, and a trial sized on the wrong rate is either underpowered or impossible to fill. B's weakness is confounding by indication, and PMID 42212353 shows how badly it can flip the direction of effect. So B should be treated as a study of feasibility and event rate, not as the answer on efficacy. Option C produces nothing, so the 2-year success criterion is missed by design. With no registered trial in Egypt or Africa, waiting brings no local data.
TOP RISK: In the retrospective data, many elective PCI patients have no creatinine at 48–72 hours. If so, neither the event rate nor the effect estimate in B can be trusted, and every later trial calculation inherits that error.
FIX: Turn B into a hybrid. Run the 2-year chart review alongside a 3–4 month prospective screening log of every diabetic elective PCI patient in a REDCap registry, with a protocol-required creatinine draw at 48–72 hours. This needs ethics approval for an observational registry with consent where required. Meanwhile, put drug and placebo sourcing and a statistician's time into the next grant application. That would raise B to 8.5. It would also raise A to about 6.0, if A is redesigned as a pilot or multicentre trial using measured numbers for eligibility, consent rate and endpoint completion.
EVIDENCE USED: PMID 42212353, PMID 37276980, NCT04806633, PMID 39890841; recruitment estimates, approval delays and drug-supply lead times are expert judgment.

## Response 2
STANCE: A, but only after a safety redesign; B adds one more confounded cohort to a pile that already conflicts, and C leaves the gap open.
SCORES: A=5.0, B=4.5, C=3.0
VETO: TRIGGERED - Option A as written gives dapagliflozin across a fasting, contrast-exposed procedure in T2DM patients with eGFR 30-60, and names ketoacidosis and volume depletion only as secondary outcomes. It has no DSMB, no stopping rules and no ketone monitoring, and one 2026 cohort shows SGLT2i use linked to HIGHER CA-AKI risk (PMID 42212353). | CLEARS IF: (1) an independent DSMB with a pre-set harm boundary on AKI and ketoacidosis, plus an interim look after 100 patients; (2) capillary ketones checked before the procedure and daily to 72 h, with a written rule to stop the drug if ketones are 1.5 mmol/L or more or if oral intake is lost (threshold is expert judgment); (3) a protocol for keeping patients hydrated and stopping the drug in the fasting window; (4) exclusion of insulin-deficient patients, patients on a ketogenic or low-carbohydrate diet, and patients with eGFR below 30 at screening; (5) before ethics submission, a written check with the NCT04806633 investigators on whether that trial finished or was abandoned.
ANALYSIS: The question is not yet answered. No adequately powered RCT with a clinical endpoint exists, and the only peri-procedural RCT (PMID 39890841, n=121) measured surrogates. The large pooled effects (RR 0.48, PMID 39392560; RR 0.37, PMID 38179307) come from observational studies of chronic users. They are very likely inflated by confounding by indication, and PMID 38179307 itself calls the evidence inconclusive. PMID 42212353 points the other way, and PMID 42123715 questions whether kidney protection applies across the whole drug class. So equipoise is real, but so is a plausible harm signal, and a 6-day course is exactly the setting where nobody has measured volume and ketone risks. On clinical meaningfulness, the pooled creatinine difference (about -10 to -14 µmol/L, PMID 38963097) is small. A KDIGO creatinine-only endpoint may detect changes patients never feel. Option B cannot resolve the conflict because chronic users differ from non-users, and C waits on a Greek RCT that may never report and on observational studies (NCT07231939).
TOP RISK: Peri-procedural fasting plus a new SGLT2i causes euglycaemic ketoacidosis or volume depletion that is not recognised, made worse by contrast, with no independent body to stop the trial.
FIX: Add the DSMB, ketone monitoring and stopping rules above, and add a patient-relevant key secondary endpoint (dialysis or persistent kidney dysfunction at 30 days). That lifts A to 7.5.
EVIDENCE USED: PMID 39392560, PMID 38179307, PMID 38963097, PMID 39890841, PMID 42212353, PMID 42123715, PMID 41751827, PMID 38230484, NCT04806633, NCT07231939; ketone threshold and fasting risk are expert judgment.

## Response 3
STANCE: Back B. It costs patients nothing and shows whether people here actually come back for a 48-72 h creatinine test, before anyone is asked to take six days of a study drug.
SCORES: A=4.5, B=6.5, C=3.0
ANALYSIS: Option A puts a heavy load on anxious patients. Each one takes dapagliflozin or placebo for three days before PCI, so they need a dispensing visit before admission. They take three more days afterwards and give blood at 48-72 h. Many people having elective PCI go home the next day (expert judgment). For them, coming back means travel, lost wages and out-of-pocket lab costs, and the people who drop out will be the poorer, older and more rural patients. Missing 72 h creatinine values will then cluster in exactly the patients most at risk of AKI (expert judgment). The eligible group is small: SGLT2i-naive, eGFR 30-60 and elective, out of roughly 400 diabetic PCIs a year. Reaching n of about 300 in 18 months with no trial coordinator means the residents and interventionalists would be consenting patients between cases, and in-lab consent rushes them (expert judgment). Peri-procedural fasting on an SGLT2i raises a real risk of euglycaemic ketoacidosis, and patients with low literacy need Arabic, spoken-word safety instructions about it (expert judgment). Randomising to placebo is acceptable to patients because no proven preventive treatment exists, and the brief already lists standard hydration and statin in both arms. The primary outcome, KDIGO AKI, is a lab definition. What patients care about is dialysis (PMID 41751827), a longer hospital stay and going home well. The brief records no patient and public involvement. Option B asks nothing of patients. It also tells the team how often 72 h creatinine is actually recorded, which is the main feasibility question for A. It cannot settle efficacy, because the observational signals point in opposite directions (PMID 39392560 vs PMID 42212353). Option C leaves Egyptian and African patients with no local evidence. The large Greek trial NCT04806633 has unknown status and has posted no results, so waiting may produce nothing.
TOP RISK: In A, many patients would not return for the 48-72 h blood draw after a next-day discharge. Retention would be uneven between groups and the primary endpoint would be missing in the highest-risk patients.
FIX: Before A, set up a patient advisory group of diabetic PCI patients and run a 3-month pilot that measures three things: the consent rate, completion of the 72 h draw when it is done at a local lab with transport reimbursed, and whether patients understand an Arabic low-literacy consent form and sick-day guidance. Add 30-day dialysis and readmission as patient-centred outcomes. This would raise A to 6.5.
EVIDENCE USED: PMID 39392560, PMID 42212353, PMID 41751827, NCT04806633

## Response 4
STANCE: A, but only after it is re-powered. Of the three options, only a randomised design avoids confounding by indication, and the n of 300 cannot answer the KDIGO question.
SCORES: A=5.5, B=3.5, C=2.5
ANALYSIS: The question is causal and about a short peri-procedural course, so it needs randomisation. B cannot answer it. B would compare chronic users with non-users, which is prevalent-user bias plus confounding by indication: users differ in eGFR trajectory, prescriber and diabetes control. The conflicting directions in the pack show this. PMID 37276980 and PMID 39392560 point one way. PMID 42212353 points the other (propensity-matched OR 2.38). Matching has not settled the direction. B also has an information-bias problem. Routine creatinine at 48 to 72 h is drawn selectively, more often in sicker patients, so KDIGO ascertainment differs between groups. The only thing B can usefully give is the control-arm event rate, and a descriptive audit would give that.
A's power is the weak point. Assume a control CA-AKI rate of 12% (PMID 42212353 reports 11.7%), two-sided alpha 0.05 and 80% power. At the meta-analytic RR of 0.5 (12% vs 6%), A needs about 355 per arm, about 780 in total with 10% loss. At a more plausible RR of 0.7, the observational estimates being inflated, it needs about 2,200. With n=300, A detects only RR ≤0.3, so a null result would be uninterpretable. The site has about 400 diabetic PCIs a year, and the eGFR 30-60, SGLT2i-naive, elective subset will probably not reach 300 in 18 months (expert judgment).
Design must-haves:
- Central web randomisation, stratified by eGFR (<45 vs 45-60) and contrast volume.
- Matched placebo, which the team has not yet secured, so blinding fails without it.
- Creatinine at 24, 48 and 72 h written into the protocol.
- Modified ITT with multiple imputation, plus a tipping-point analysis.
- Secondary endpoints labelled exploratory; the safety outcomes are not hypothesis tests.
- Report the protocol to SPIRIT 2025 and the trial to CONSORT 2025.
C answers nothing. NCT04806633 is the only trial in the pack, its status is unknown and it uses a 5 mg dose.
TOP RISK: An underpowered single-centre trial gives an imprecise null result (say RR 0.7, 95% CI 0.35 to 1.4) and closes the question locally for years.
FIX: Make A a multicentre RCT across 3 or 4 Egyptian cath labs. Base the sample size on a control rate measured in a 3-month local audit, with a target of about 780 for RR 0.5. If that cannot be funded, register the 300-patient study openly as a pilot feasibility RCT, not an efficacy trial. Either route raises A to 7.5.
EVIDENCE USED: PMID 42212353, PMID 39392560, PMID 38179307, PMID 37276980, PMID 39890841, NCT04806633

## Response 5
STANCE: Back B. The approval path is short and lawful, and its results give A the equipoise and safety case it needs before regulators review it.
SCORES: A=5.5, B=8.0, C=5.0
VETO: NONE
ANALYSIS: A is an interventional drug trial for a new indication: a short peri-procedural course of dapagliflozin to prevent CA-AKI. It needs full review, not expedited review. Under Law 214/2020, the institutional REC must approve it, then the Supreme Council for review of clinical research. The Egyptian Drug Authority (EDA) must approve the investigational use and the matching placebo, which has not yet been secured. Without a placebo source and its manufacturing (GMP) documents, the EDA file cannot even be submitted. A 4-month "approved and registered" target is not realistic for this path (expert judgment). The pack also shows a real safety question. PMID 42212353 links SGLT2i use to higher CI-AKI risk (propensity-matched OR 2.38), and PMID 42123715 questions class-wide renoprotection. So A's protocol needs an independent DSMB, a harm stopping rule, and consent wording that does not repeat the "large benefit" claim (PMIDs 39392560, 38179307). Consent must be taken in writing well before the 3-day pre-PCI start. It must not be taken at the cath-lab booking by the treating cardiologist, because patients depend on that person for their care and may feel unable to say no. A must be prospectively registered on PACTR or ClinicalTrials.gov before the first patient is enrolled. There is no trial in Egypt or Africa (pack gaps), which supports the case but does not shorten approval. B is a records review. The REC can approve it with a waiver of consent if: re-contacting patients is impracticable, risk is minimal, and data are coded at extraction. Health data are sensitive data under PDPL 151/2020, so data handling needs the data controller's authorisation and no transfer outside Egypt. The REC must confirm whether Law 214 applies to B. C carries no regulatory risk. But it leaves an unregistered evidence gap and plans no Egyptian contribution. NCT04806633 has status unknown, so it may never report.
TOP RISK: A is submitted with no secured placebo supply and no statistician. The EDA or the Supreme Council returns it, and the team then switches to an open-label or unblinded design that the REC never approved.
FIX: Run B first under a REC-approved consent waiver with a data-protection plan. In parallel, secure an independent drug-and-placebo supply with a written conflict-of-interest declaration, and appoint a DSMB. With those in place, A rises to 7.5.
EVIDENCE USED: PMID 42212353, PMID 42123715, PMID 39392560, PMID 38179307, NCT04806633; the regulatory route and timeline points are expert judgment only. The REC, the Supreme Council, the EDA and the data protection authority must each confirm their own points.
