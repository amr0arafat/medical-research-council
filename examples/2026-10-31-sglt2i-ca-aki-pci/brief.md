# Brief: SGLT2 inhibitors to prevent contrast-associated AKI after elective PCI

Type: protocol · Mode: full · Date: 2026-10-31 · TEST RUN (hypothetical project)

## Decision
How should an interventional cardiology team at an Egyptian university hospital (assumed) study whether an SGLT2 inhibitor prevents contrast-associated acute kidney injury (CA-AKI) in patients with type 2 diabetes undergoing elective PCI?

## Options
- **A. Single-centre double-blind RCT now.** SGLT2i-naive adults with T2DM and eGFR 30-60 (assumed) scheduled for elective PCI; dapagliflozin 10 mg vs placebo from 3 days before to 3 days after PCI (assumed), on top of standard hydration. Primary endpoint: KDIGO AKI within 72 h. Planned n of about 300 (assumed), 18 months.
- **B. Retrospective cohort first.** Two years of the hospital's PCI records: T2DM patients already on an SGLT2i vs not, propensity-matched, KDIGO AKI at 48-72 h. Use it to estimate the local event rate and effect size, then decide on an RCT.
- **C. Do not proceed.** Wait for larger ongoing studies.

## PICO
- P: adults with T2DM, eGFR 30-60, elective PCI (assumed).
- I: dapagliflozin 10 mg, short peri-procedural course.
- C: placebo plus standard care (hydration, statin).
- O: KDIGO AKI within 72 h; secondary creatinine and cystatin C change, dialysis, 30-day MACE, euglycaemic ketoacidosis, genital infection, volume depletion.
- T: 72 h primary, 30 days secondary.

## Setting and team
- Egypt; university hospital cath lab doing roughly 1,200 PCIs a year, of which about a third have diabetes (assumed).
- Team: two interventional cardiologists, one nephrologist, a resident; no dedicated statistician or trial coordinator (assumed).
- Funding: a small internal faculty grant; drug and matching placebo not yet secured (assumed).
- Approvals: institutional research ethics committee; Egyptian law on clinical medical research applies to an interventional drug study.

## Success
Protocol approved and registered within 4 months; a publishable primary result within 2 years.

## The lean
The team leans to **A**, because two meta-analyses suggest a large benefit and an editorial asks whether it is "time for a PCI trial".
